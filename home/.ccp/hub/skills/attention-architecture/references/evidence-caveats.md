# Evidence caveats

Load when a claim needs its evidence strength or before you build a rule on a famous effect. Grades (`S`, `A`, `B`, `H`) are in [../SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), which also carries the Tier 3 list. `P` numbers are internal: to a user, cite author and year.

**Answering "is this effect real?":** give the verdict first, then the one or two studies that decide it (author, year, design, size), what the effect still supports if anything, and what the user can honestly say or do instead. Say when you recall a figure from memory. ## How to judge a claim

1. **Find the design.** Meta-analysis or multi-lab replication > preregistered field test > single lab study > review or theory paper > practitioner blog. A neuroimaging finding shows where something happens, not that a design rule works.
2. **Check what was measured.** Sharing is not clicking; liking is not attention; recall in a lab is not retention on a platform; a model's predicted "importance" is not gaze.
3. **Check the sample's boundary.** Most of this evidence is English-speaking, Western, 1990s–2010s, often students or one publisher (Upworthy, NYT). Say so when you apply it to Vietnamese, B2B or a different platform.
4. **Look for the replication.** Many famous effects shrank or vanished (below). If you cannot find one, call it single-study (Tier 2), not a law.
5. **Do not trust a precise number that has no source.**

## Tiers with sources

### Tier 1: use freely

| Effect | Source | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Anchoring | Many Labs 1 (`P35`, Klein 2014) | Implausible anchors invite counterargument |
| Natural frequencies beat percentages for risk | `P48` Gigerenzer & Hoffrage 1995: Bayesian accuracy 15.9% → 45.8%; meta-analysis OR ≈ 7.6 (McDowell & Jacobs 2017) | One shared denominator |
| Endorsed defaults | `P43` Jachimowicz 2019 (d ≈ 0.68); field evidence (pensions, organ donation) | Bias-corrected nudge average is near zero (`P40` Maier 2022); defaults are the exception, not proof that nudges work |
| Concreteness | `P53` Packard & Berger 2020 (service replies: satisfaction and spend up); `P51`, `P52` | Abstract wording fits "why" framing (`P54`) |
| Narrative transportation | `P49b` van Laer 2014 (76 studies, r = .41 attitude) | Needs a real plot, not a one-line quote |
| High-arousal emotion raises sharing | `P55` Berger & Milkman 2012 (NYT most-emailed, 2008) | Sharing only. Clicks behave differently (`P14`) |
| Repetition raises belief | `P50` Dechêne 2010 | Belief, not attention. Repeating a myth strengthens it |
| A threat message needs a doable action | Tannenbaum et al. 2015 meta-analysis of fear appeals | Fear without a way to act can backfire |
| One all-in price | `P65` Thaler 1985 (combined losses hurt less); the trust cost of fees found late | Drip fees can raise short-term demand (Morwitz 1998), so the case is trust |
| Position beats angle in charts | Cleveland & McGill 1984; Heer & Bostock 2010 | Pies are acceptable for 2–3 part-to-whole slices |
| Seductive details hurt learning | `P31` Harp & Mayer 1998; Sundararajan & Adesope 2020 meta (small to moderate) | Tangents after the payoff are fine |
| Mayer's signaling, contiguity, redundancy | Mayer's multimedia principles; Schneider 2018 meta (signaling) | Learning content; captions are an accessibility need, not redundancy |
| Peak and end dominate remembered experience | `P34` Kahneman 1993 | Borderline p in the original; the direction is well replicated |

### Tier 2: use inside the boundary and say it

| Effect | Source and caveat |
|---|---|
| Precise-number anchors | Janiszewski & Uy 2008, one paper; precise numbers raise credibility, round ones are easier to remember |
| "I" over "we" in service replies | `P56` Packard 2018 (retail emails + experiments). Extending it to landing pages or to Vietnamese pronouns is extrapolation |
| Specific negative words raise headline clicks | `P14` Robertson 2023, registered report on Upworthy: each negative word about +2.3% CTR, positive words lower it. Fear words *lowered* CTR, anger was null, sadness raised it. Headline length had a larger effect |
| English feed-headline patterns | Repo analysis of Upworthy's 2013–15 US social-news A/B tests (headline-patterns.md). English social headlines only |
| Questions in headlines | Won 31% of Upworthy pairs (English feed headlines; headline-patterns.md); Lai & Farbrot 2014 found self-referencing questions helped; Petty 1981 (`P64`, one lab study, N = 160): rhetorical questions help with weak involvement and hurt with high involvement |
| Endowed progress | Nunes & Drèze 2006, one car-wash study; credit real steps only |
| Daily-price framing | `P67` Gourville 1998: helped at about $1/day (marginal, p < .06), hurt at $4/day; cells of 15–25 people; 1998 dollars |
| Loss aversion | `P44` Brown 2024: λ about 1.5–2 depending on adjustment and elicitation; stake size had no clear effect in a later re-analysis. Use for endowment framing only |
| Decoy | `P45` Frederick 2014: 4 of 5 abstract numeric matrices, 0 of 27 perceptual; Yang & Lynn 2014: 11 of 91 |
| Choice overload | `P46` Chernev 2015 (N = 7,202): four independent moderators; Scheibehenne 2010: mean ≈ 0 |
| IKEA effect | `P47` Norton 2012: finished builds only |
| "I don't" vs "I can't" | `P57` Patrick & Hagtvedt 2012: the persistence study had N = 30; no independent replication found |
| Pratfall effect | Aronson 1966; Ein-Gar 2012: only after competence is shown |
| Syntax locality | `P61`–`P63` (Levy 2008, Jaeger 2010, Gibson 1998): direction is solid; "≤ 5 words subject to verb" is a heuristic |
| Interest needs novelty plus coping potential | `P23` Silvia 2008 (review); `P24` Hidi & Renninger 2006: catching interest does not hold it without relevance |

### Tier 3: never use as a claim or rationale

| Claim | Why |
|---|---|
| Ego depletion | `P38` Hagger 2016 multi-lab RRR: d ≈ 0.04 |
| Signing at the top raises honesty | Shu 2012, retracted |
| Money, flag and other social priming | `P35` Many Labs 1 failures; elderly priming failed in Doyen 2012 |
| Power posing | Ranehill 2015 failed replication |
| Pen-in-mouth facial feedback | Wagenmakers 2016 RRR null. Other facial-feedback methods show small effects (Coles 2022), so claim nothing for design |
| Unconscious thought theory | Nieuwenstein 2015 null |
| Rhyme-as-reason | De Keersmaecker 2021: d ≈ 0.02 |
| Red/blue raise accuracy or creativity | `P01` Mehta & Zhu 2009; Steele 2014 direct replication: color explained under 1% of variance |
| Disfluent fonts improve reasoning | Meyer et al. 2015: 17 studies, null |
| Identity nouns raise behavior ("be a voter") | `P58` Bryan 2011 (small samples); Gerber 2016 PNAS field replication, n = 4,468: 30.1% vs 31.1%, p = .45; two later nulls |
| Identifiable victim; "statistics kill sympathy" | Small 2007; Lee & Feeley 2016 meta r = .05; Maier 2023 registered report null |
| Fixed East vs West scanpaths | Chua 2005 not replicated (Rayner 2007; Evans 2009) |
| Dunning-Kruger quartile chart | `P72` Gignac & Zajenkowski 2020: an artefact of regression to the mean |
| Brain-mechanism names as proof of a style rule | MMN, amygdala, dopamine and V1 explanations in older drafts had no study linking them to the rule |
