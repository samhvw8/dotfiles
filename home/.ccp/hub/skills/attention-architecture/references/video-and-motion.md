# Video and motion

Load for a short or long video script, a video storyboard, B-roll planning or motion graphics. [../SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) owns the grades (`S`, `A`, `B`, `H`), the defaults and the checks; "the SKILL default" means the value there. ## 1. Rules for every format

| Rule | Grade and source | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Open on the subject.** Frame 1 shows the problem, the result or the action in motion, and the first words come at once. Greeting, logo bumper, sponsor and subscribe ask come after the hook | H: platform practitioner data | No controlled study. Do not quote a bounce percentage for it |
| **Change of emotion holds viewers better than a constant high.** Build in rises and drops (surprise, then calm proof, then a new surprise) | B: Teixeira, Wedel & Pieters 2012, ad viewing with eye tracking and facial coding | Measured on TV ads and zapping, not on full-length content |
| **Match cut pace to load.** Cuts within one scene (angle changes) can be fast. New-scene cuts, or fast cutting under emotional content, past about 20 cuts per minute (a cut under every 3 s) lowered recognition memory. Cut fast in montage and emotional beats; keep the picture calm and continuous during dense explanation | B: Lang's LC4MP; Lang et al. 1999; Collier 2010 | Lab studies on TV material. The direction is robust; the exact rate is not |
| **No seductive details before the payoff.** Interesting but off-point material (a side story, a funny clip, decorative B-roll) placed before or inside the explanation lowers recall of the main point. The same material placed after the payoff did no harm | A: Harp & Mayer 1998, Exp. 4; Sundararajan & Adesope 2020 meta-analysis (small to moderate effect) | Tested on learning material. For pure entertainment, a tangent can be the content |
| **Narrate while you show.** The visual and the words that explain it appear together, not one after the other | A: Mayer's temporal contiguity principle | The principle compares together vs. separate segments; it gives no millisecond window |
| **No paragraph on screen that repeats the narration word for word.** Show 2–4 keywords next to the object they name instead | A: Mayer's redundancy and spatial contiguity principles; H for the word count | Closed captions are not a violation; video needs them (G13) |
| **Mark big scene changes.** When 3 or more of place, time, character, goal and cause change at once, add a chapter card or a spoken signpost. A key frame placed just after a boundary is remembered better than one inside a long scene | B: Zacks et al. 2007 review of event segmentation; H for the 3-change trigger | The memory benefit is from lab studies on everyday-activity films |
| **Vary shot length.** Avoid cutting on a fixed beat; let shots breathe after a key moment | H | Cutting 2010 describes Hollywood shot patterns. It measured no viewer outcome, so it is not evidence for a cut rule |
| **Mix talking head with demonstration.** Cut between the speaker and hands-on footage, screens or diagrams. Shorter videos held viewers better | B: Guo, Kim & Rubin 2014, MOOC lecture logs (engagement fell after about 6 min) | Lecture videos on edX. No fixed switch interval comes from this study |
| **Put the payoff and the end at the SKILL default positions.** Close on one concrete action, then stop | H for the position; A for peak-end | Long form can carry several peaks; the last one still needs the strongest proof |

## 2. Short form (15–60 s)

| Beat | What happens | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | The subject on screen in motion: the problem, or the product doing its job. The first spoken words start at once. A short on-screen line carries the hook for muted viewers | Hook rules and window sizes: hooks-and-wording.md. For a product, the working demo in frame 1 is the hook |
| 3–10 s | The pain in one concrete scene the viewer recognises, or the promise of what they will see. Name the product while the question is still open | H |
| Body | Show, don't tell: 2–4 beats, each with a visible change. Include the benefit the viewer gets later (not only features) and an answer to the main objection this audience has. Vary shot length | Cut fast only when the content is visual; slow down for any spoken explanation |
| Payoff | The concrete result (a number, a before/after, the finished thing) at the SKILL payoff position | One payoff. Do not stack 3 claims in the last 5 s |
| End | One action ("Search <app name>", "Comment your answer"). An optional loop: the last line leads back into the first frame | H for the loop |

Captions: burned-in, a few words per screen, synced to the voice, clear of the app's buttons and caption area. Muted viewing varies by platform: TikTok describes itself as sound-on, while Facebook-style feeds are often watched muted. Make the hook work both ways and do not state a muted-viewing share (H).

Show only what the product really does, and tell the user which demo claims must be true before filming. Numbers shown in a demo are real and plausible for the audience.

## 3. Long form (5 min and up)

- **Cold open (H).** Show the payoff, the problem or the most striking moment first. The title card and the intro come after. Promise what the viewer gets and when.
- **Overlapping questions (H).** Open the next question before you close the current one, so each segment hands attention to the next.
- **Pattern change at the SKILL default interval (H).** Use the V1–V5 generator below. Every change must serve the thesis, or it becomes a seductive detail.
- **Abstract, then concrete, then abstract (A for concreteness).** State the rule, show one named, specific case of exactly that rule, then return to the rule. A random example that only looks related does not count.
- **Chapters (S for platform rules, H for content).** Split at real topic changes. Give each chapter a title that names its payoff, not its topic ("Why the index never helped", not "Indexes"). Platform chapter rules are in [platform-specs.md](platform-specs.md).
- **Recaps (H).** In pieces over about 15 min, say in one sentence where the argument stands before each new major part.
- **Ending (H).** End on the final payoff and one action. Put sponsor reads and channel housekeeping before the last act or in the end screen, never after the final line.

## 4. Motion graphics and kinetic type

| Rule | Grade and source |
|---|---|
| One focal motion group per beat. Dim or hold everything else still. Motion onset captures attention, so two moving groups compete | A: Abrams & Christ 2003 (motion onset captures attention); H for one group per beat |
| Duration and easing: the SKILL Motion default. No flashing faster than 3 per second. Provide a reduced-motion version for web embeds | S: WCAG 2.3.1, 2.3.3; H for durations |
| Kinetic text and highlight rings appear on the word they illustrate, within a few frames | A: temporal contiguity; H for "a few frames" |
| During explanation, morph or reveal on one continuous canvas instead of cutting to a new scene | B: Lang's LC4MP |
| Impact sound effects land on the impact frame or up to 1 frame after it. In-event sync follows the ITU limits in platform-specs.md | S for the sync limit; H for the 1-frame rule |
| Keep text on screen long enough to read twice | H |

## 5. Pattern-change generator (V1–V5)

A pattern change resets attention only when it is tied to the thesis. A change for its own sake is a seductive detail (G10). Pick the vector that serves the current point.

| Vector | What changes | Video move | Audio move | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1 Subtraction** | Density drops | Cut to one clean frame or a black card with one line | Duck the music bed; keep room tone; pause before the key line | Use it on the key line only. Repeated, it stops working |
| **V2 Abstraction jump** | Abstract rule ↔ concrete case | Rule on screen, then a named person or object that shows exactly that rule | Rule, then a short scene with a name, a place and a number | The case must have the same structure as the rule |
| **V3 Cross-modal lead** | Sound leads picture | J-cut: the next scene's sound starts before the picture cut. L-cut: the sound carries over the cut | A sound cue or a question crosses into the next segment | A J-cut is an editing choice. It is not the AV sync limit, which applies inside one event |
| **V4 Framing change** | Scale or frame | Wide to extreme close-up of the exact flaw, the hand, the number | Room sound to close-mic voice | Zoom onto the thing the point is about, never for motion alone |
| **V5 Agency flip** | Viewer predicts | Show option A and option B for a few seconds ("Which one failed?"), then reveal | Ask the listener to guess, then answer within about 15 s | One grounded question, answered soon. A string of open questions reads as filler |

## 6. Attention flavors (F1–F6)

Pick one primary flavor before you script. The flavors come from correlations between script features and audience rating tags across about 2,000 TED talks in the research repo (grade B: one corpus, many tests, correlational). Use them to frame choices, not as laws.

| Flavor | Best for | Opening move | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1 Curiosity** | Explainers, demos, technical deep dives | Open on the puzzling artifact or result. A personal backstory first weakens this flavor; get to the mechanism within the first 20% | The mechanism, shown working |
| **F2 Awe** | Brand films, showcases, launches | Open on scale or a striking image; alternate close-ups with wide frames | The largest reveal, then quiet |
| **F3 Conviction** | Investigations, safety or fraud alerts, policy | Open on one named case and its concrete loss | A doable action. Fear without a way to act backfires (A: Tannenbaum et al. 2015 meta-analysis on fear appeals) |
| **F4 Elevation** | Founder and customer stories, profiles | Open in the middle of a human scene | A fall-then-rise arc ending in what changed |
| **F5 Warmth and humor** | Creator reels, conversational shows | A setup–punch rhythm: medium setups, short punch lines | Short. Stop on the last laugh or the warm moment |
| **F6 Boredom and overload** | A failure state, never a goal | Signs: abstract wording, strings of questions, run-on sentences, overlong runtime | Fix with concrete cases, cuts, and a shorter runtime |

This settles when to open on a human story (G14): open on a person for F3–F5. For F1, open on the artifact or the puzzle; a person may appear, but the mechanism must arrive early.

## 7. Color script

- Keep one stable grade (the carrier) across the piece. Shift it only at chapter boundaries, never inside a scene (H).
- Reserve the single accent hue (SKILL Accent default) for the peak and the final call to action (H).
- Caption pills: dark pill, light text, keyword in the accent. Compute every pair against the SKILL contrast defaults; the worked values are in SKILL.
- Do not justify a grade with color-emotion science. Lighter and more saturated colors tend to read as more positive, darker and greyer as more negative (B); everything finer is taste.

## 8. Common failures

- A greeting, logo or "in this video I will…" before the hook.
- A story or explainer that gives its answer away in the first sentence (a product demo is the exception: show it working at once).
- A tangent, joke or stock clip placed before the payoff.
- A paragraph on screen that repeats the narration, or no closed captions at all.
- Fast cutting while the narration explains something hard.
- Flashing montages faster than 3 flashes per second.
- Captions or the face under the platform UI.
- A product video that tours features and never shows the pain, the benefit or the objection.
- A brain-mechanism name (MMN, dopamine, amygdala) used as the reason for an editing choice.
- An ending that trails into housekeeping after the payoff.
