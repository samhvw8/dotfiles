# Hooks and wording

Load for a hook, headline, title, CTA, microcopy or any copywriting task, in any medium and language. Grades (`S`/`A`/`B`/`H`), defaults and checks are in [../SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Win-rate data for English feed headlines is in [headline-patterns.md](headline-patterns.md); load it only for that job.

## Writing a hook

Practitioner rules (H) unless a line says otherwise. They hold across media; the user's own test data overrides them.

- **Start from the audience's situation.** Name the pain they already recognise or the result they want, in the words they would use. Lead with the outcome and let the mechanism follow: "Invoices that chase themselves" beats a sentence that describes the reminder engine.
- **A product you can demonstrate: the product working is the hook.** Show it doing the job in the first second or the first screen. The proof then sits inside the hook, and the open question moves to the viewer's next doubt ("does it work in the hard case?"), which the body answers.
- **A story or an explainer: open on the symptom, the striking moment or the result.** Keep the cause for the body. This "do not give the answer away" rule applies to story and explainer content only, never to a product demo.
- **Be specific.** One person, one thing, one number, one moment. The creator's own real number beats a borrowed statistic.
- **Use the hook-form default.** A question works only when the audience cannot answer it at once and the piece answers it. A question everyone answers "yes" to in their head, or a string of them, is filler.
- **Fit the window.** Time a spoken hook at the SKILL speech-rate default. A 3 s window holds about 8–9 English words, or fewer than about 15 Vietnamese syllables read flat out, and fewer still around an action or a pause. When it runs over, cut words; do not speed up.
- **Make it work with sound off and with sound on.** The picture plus a short on-screen line carries it alone, and so does the spoken line.
- **Length follows the slot, not a word count.** A hero headline reads at a glance: one line for the outcome, one support line for how. Do not pad a headline to reach a length.
- **Pay it off.** The piece delivers what the hook promised, early. A hook built on an invented number or anecdote is bait and costs the brand trust.
- **Match the message.** A headline reuses the promise of the ad, query or post that brought the visitor.

**When asked for several hooks,** make each a different bet: the demonstration, the question they cannot answer, the pain scene they recognise (a POV), the surprising real number, the story moment. Pick one, say why for this audience and platform, name its weak point, and say how the rest of the piece covers it.

**A piece that sells a product** needs more than a demo. A reliable arc: the hook, the pain in a concrete scene, the product working, the payoff the user gets later (the benefit, not the feature), the main objection answered, one action.

**CTA.** The label says what happens next or what the person gets ("Start free, no card", not "Learn more"). One ask per CTA.

## Wording rules

| Rule | Do this | Grade, source and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Concreteness** | Name things a person can see or touch ("grey cotton shirt", "4-line SQL query"), with action verbs ("shipped", "signed"). Replace bare pro-forms ("this issue", "our solution", "the platform") and stock claims any competitor could make ("comprehensive", "leading AI") | A: Packard & Berger 2020 (concrete service replies raised satisfaction and spend); Hansen & Wänke 2010 (concrete wording raises judged truth) |
| **Plain words** | The common word over the impressive one ("use", not "leverage") | A: Oppenheimer 2006 (needless complexity lowers judged intelligence) |
| **Fear needs a way out** | If the hook names a threat, the payoff gives a concrete, doable step | A: Tannenbaum et al. 2015 meta-analysis |
| **Gain vs loss framing** | Do not expect a lift from rewording a benefit as a loss | A: O'Keefe & Jensen meta-analyses, near-zero difference |
| **Self-reference** | Tie the claim to the reader's own situation ("your last deploy", "the app on your phone") | A for memory: Symons & Johnson 1997 meta-analysis. B for clicks |
| **Pronouns (English)** | Support, founder and creator replies say "I", not "we"; "we" is for company policy | B: Packard, Moore & McFerran 2018 (service emails). Extending it to landing copy is an extrapolation |
| **Error messages** | Say what happened and the way forward. Never blame the reader ("The card didn't go through", not "You entered an invalid card") | H |
| **Syntax** | Keep the subject close to its verb. No clauses nested inside clauses. Put the payoff word at the end of the sentence | A for the direction: Gibson 1998, Jaeger 2010 |
| **Rhetorical questions in body copy** | Possible for a cold, low-involvement reader with strong proof behind them. Never on pricing, checkout or docs | B: Petty, Cacioppo & Heesacker 1981, one lab study; later studies are mixed |
| **Private by default** | Where users fear being watched or exposed (money, health, drafts, recordings), say who can see it, only if it is true | B: spotlight effect (Gilovich et al. 2000) |
| **Styled claims with no support** | Identity nouns ("be a builder"), "but you are free to…" and "I don't" over "I can't" are fine as style. Promise no lift from them | Tier 3 or weak: see evidence-caveats.md |

## Vietnamese

No wording rule here has a Vietnamese test, so these are practice (H).

- **Write it fresh.** Compose in Vietnamese from the idea. A sentence translated from an English pattern reads stiff. Use the audience's own slang and loanwords where they do ("deal", "ship", "check-in").
- **Voice.** `mình` and `bạn` for a friendly creator or brand; `tôi` for a formal or expert voice; `em` with `anh/chị` in one-to-one service. Written brand copy keeps one voice. Billing, consent and legal lines use the neutral company voice (the brand name or `chúng tôi`), not `mình`.
- **Speech is looser.** A host who moves between `mình`, `các bạn` and `chúng ta` is speaking normally. Leave it.
- **Length and time** are counted in syllables (SKILL speech-rate default).
- **Numbers** read aloud are written as words in a script ("một nghìn hai trăm", not "1.200"), so the host does not stumble.

## Before and after

The numbers are illustrations. Real copy uses the user's real figures.

| Context | Before | After | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| SaaS hero | *"The comprehensive data operations optimization solution for modern enterprises."* / *"Giải pháp tối ưu hóa quy trình vận hành dữ liệu toàn diện cho doanh nghiệp hiện đại."* | *"Merge 14 scattered spreadsheets into one reconciliation screen in 90 seconds."* / *"Gom 14 bảng tính rời rạc về một màn hình đối soát trong 90 giây."* | Abstract nouns replaced by things you can see, an action verb and an exact number |
| Pricing header | *"Don't you think upgrading to Pro would save your company money?"* | *"Pro shuts down idle servers at 10 PM. Customers cut their monthly AWS bill by 38% on average."* / *"Gói Pro tự tắt máy chủ nhàn rỗi lúc 22h. Khách hàng giảm trung bình 38% hóa đơn AWS mỗi tháng."* | A rhetorical question on a high-intent page replaced by a statement with a real figure |
| Short video hook | *"Today I'll share some nice tips to write better Python."* / *"Hôm nay mình chia sẻ vài mẹo hay để viết Python tốt hơn."* | *"These 3 pandas lines are eating 16 GB of your RAM. Here's the 5-second fix."* / *"3 dòng pandas này đang ngốn 16 GB RAM của bạn. Sửa mất đúng 5 giây."* | Greeting and vague praise replaced by a specific costly outcome and a way out |
| Case-study hook | *"Company X is the regional leader in delivery quality."* | *"At 5:00 every morning, 1,400 X drivers scan fresh-milk crates onto shelves before sunrise."* / *"5 giờ sáng mỗi ngày, 1.400 tài xế của X quét mã từng thùng sữa tươi lên kệ trước khi trời sáng."* | A claimed status replaced by people doing an observable action |
| Payment error | *"ERROR: You entered an invalid card. Update it now to avoid suspension."* | *"The bank didn't approve the charge on card ••4821. Your project data is safe. Try another card or pay by QR below."* / *"Ngân hàng chưa xác nhận giao dịch thẻ ••4821. Dữ liệu dự án của bạn vẫn còn nguyên. Bạn thử thẻ khác hoặc chuyển khoản QR bên dưới."* | Blame and threat removed; the data is safe; two ways forward |
| Service reply | *"We have processed your request."* | *"I checked order #402 and sent a new grey shirt, size L, by express. It arrives before 3 PM tomorrow."* / *"Mình đã kiểm tra đơn #402 và gửi lại áo thun xám size L bằng hỏa tốc, tới trước 15h mai."* | "I", the specific item, the action and the time |
| Nested sentence | *"The analytics report that the data team that the firm outsourced designed for executives last month loads slowly."* | *"The outsourced team built this executive report last month, and each page now takes 18 seconds to load."* | Nested clauses removed; subject next to verb; the number at the end |
