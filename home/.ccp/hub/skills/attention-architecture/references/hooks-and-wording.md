**Guard clauses (adversarial red team):**

1. **Guard Clause 1 (No Interrogative Termination on Curiosity Triggers):** If a hook contains `why|what|how|who|this`, it MUST NOT end with `?` (`Guarded wins 67.51%`, $z = +6.98, N = 397$).
2. **Guard Clause 2 (Mandatory Concrete Anchor for Paradoxes):** If a hook contains a contrast/paradox pivot (`H06`), it MUST co-occur with `H04` (Human Protagonist) or `H09` (Sensory Artifact) (`Guarded wins 61.42%`, $z = +2.57, N = 127$).
3. **Guard Clause 3 (Scene Before Question Word):** Never open a `<10`-word hook with bare `"How To..."` or `"Why..."`; lead with the concrete entity or counter-intuitive action (`Guarded wins 71.53%`, $z = +5.04, N = 137$).
4. **Guard Clause 4 (Deadpan Delivery on High-Arousal Stakes):** When `H05` (Negativity/Threat) or `H10` (High-Arousal Awe/Outrage) is active, forbid `!` and `ALL-CAPS` words (`|z| = 3.52`).

## Empirical basis and top archetypes (45,246 A/B packages)

Trained on `22,656` Exploratory A/B packages (`OSF 3vqmp`) and verified zero-leakage on `22,590` untouched Holdout A/B packages (`OSF ynf3k`, `6,782` statistically significant pairs at $|z| \ge 1.96$; `68.30%` pairwise winner accuracy, $z = +30.14, p = 1.51 \times 10^{-199}$; `70.27%` on $|z| \ge 2.58$):

### 5.1 Top Empirical Hook Archetypes (`H01..H12`)
1. **`H01` Cataphoric Referent Delay (`67.16%` Exploratory / `63.21%` Holdout win rate, `+20.4% to +26.1%` CTR):** Assert a concrete referent exists while withholding its identity (*"This One..."*, *"Here's What Happened When..."*, *"The Moment..."*).
2. **`H03` Temporal-Causal Event Pivot (`62.21%` Exploratory / `68.42%` Holdout win rate, `+18.2% to +27.7%` CTR):** Trigger Event Segmentation (`Zacks et al. 2007`) via unfolding temporal boundaries (*"When He..."*, *"Until..."*, *"In 60 Seconds..."*).
3. **`H02` Subitizable Quantified Singularity (`62.86%` Exploratory / `58.11%` Holdout win rate, `+11.4% to +19.2%` CTR):** Anchor on pre-attentive cardinalities `1–4` (*"1 Coffee Shop"*, *"2 Words"*, *"3 Seconds"*).
4. **`H09` Sensory Media Artifact Anchor (`62.40%` Exploratory / `60.41%` Holdout win rate, `+16.9% to +19.1%` CTR):** Name a concrete visual/auditory object (*"Video/Photo/Chart/Map/Letter/Watch/See/Prove"*).
5. **`H04` Human Protagonist Agency (`59.17%` Exploratory / `57.38%` Holdout win rate, `+11.1% to +13.3%` CTR):** Anchor in a human social agent (*"He/She/They"*, *"A Mom/Teacher/CEO/Engineer"*).
6. **`H05` Negativity & Threat Salience (`58.97%` Exploratory / `56.66%` Holdout win rate, `+10.5% to +13.8%` CTR):** Use concrete threat/mistake framing (*"Worst/Wrong/Never/Stop/Mistake/Nightmare"*).
7. **Synergy Rule (`2–4` Archetypes):** Combine `2–4` complementary archetypes in a `12–18` word (`65–100` char) declarative hook (`+0.35` log-odds bonus).

## Four copywriting myths refuted by randomized A/B data

- **Myth 1 (`FMEA-01`, RPN `504`): Question Marks (`?`) at End of Hook — LOSES `69.73%` of A/B tests (`30.27%` win rate, `-31.91%` CTR, $z = -12.48$):** Invites a silent *"No"* (Betteridge's Law). Even when a curiosity word (*"why/what/who"*) is present, adding `?` causes it to **lose `67.51%` of the time** ($z = -6.98$) against a declarative statement!
- **Myth 2 (`FMEA-02`, RPN `384`): Exclamation Marks (`!`) & `ALL-CAPS` Shouting — LOSES `70.45%` (`!`, $z = -6.43$) and `60.97%` (`ALL-CAPS`, $z = -3.60$):** Triggers persuasion-knowledge reactance and Cry-Wolf alarm fatigue (`Patterson 1982`, `Edworthy et al. 1991`).
- **Myth 3 (`FMEA-03`, RPN `336`): Ultra-Short Hooks (`<8` Words) — LOSES `70.62%` of A/B tests (`29.38%` win rate, `-30.95%` CTR, $z = -5.74$):** Context starvation prevents mental-model formation.
- **Myth 4 (`FMEA-04`, RPN `294`): Unanchored Abstract Paradox — LOSES `61.42%` ($z = -2.57$):** Every paradox (`H06`: *"but/actually/instead"*) MUST be anchored to a Human Protagonist (`H04`) or Sensory Artifact (`H09`) and must withhold the resolution until after the click/cut.

## Sentence-length cadence and run-on toxicity

In our empirical evaluation of **2,465 full-length TED Talks** (`1,970` Exploratory + `495` untouched Holdout, `6,009,876` audience ratings):
- **Balanced Sentence Cadence (`balanced_rhythm`):** Alternating short staccato punches (`<= 7 words`) with medium narrative clauses (`10–22 words`) strongly drives **Social Warmth & Levity (`Funny`: $r = +0.217, z = +9.9, p < 10^{-21}$)** and reduces `Longwinded` boredom.
- **Run-On Sentence Toxicity (`runon_share`):** Sentences exceeding `32–35 words` overload the phonological loop (`Giraud & Poeppel, 2012`), significantly increasing **`Longwinded` boredom ($r = +0.090, z = +3.99, p < 0.0001$)** and destroying **`Funny / Levity` ($r = -0.172, z = -7.8, p < 10^{-14}$)**.

## The 8 first-principles laws of attention-grabbing wording (`P51–P64`)

### Law 1: Sensorimotor Concreteness & The Listening Inference (`P51–P54`)
- **Empirical Mechanism:** Words referring to tangible, visually or haptically perceptible entities ($C \ge 4.0$ on Brysbaert's 1–5 scale, `P52`) activate dual verbal (`logogen`) + sensorimotor (`imagen`) codes (`Clark & Paivio 1991, P51`), co-activating visual ($r = +0.449$) and motor/haptic ($r = +0.410$) cortices.
- **Quantitative Payoff:**
  - **`-40 to -80 ms` faster lexical recognition** and **`~2×` memory recall** over abstract words (`P51, P54`).
  - **`+8.9%` higher customer satisfaction** ($t = 2.36, p = .019$) and **`+13% to +30%` higher 90-day spending** (`+$4.00 to +$10.07`, $p = .004$) per `+1 SD` (`+5.6%`) increase in lexical concreteness, mediated by **perceived listening** ($F(1,204) = 5.74, p = .017$) rather than processing fluency (`Packard & Berger 2020, P53`).
  - **Higher subjective truth ratings** ($\eta_p^2 = .081\text{–}.19, p \le .004$, `Hansen & Wänke 2010, P54`) when facts use **Descriptive Action Verbs (DAV: *wrote, signed, counted, shipped*)** instead of abstract state verbs or nominalizations (*is by, holds the record, optimization*).
- **Execution Rule:** Ban empty pro-forms (*this, that, it, issue, item, solution, platform, optimize*) when a concrete noun + action verb exists (*grey cotton T-shirt, 4-line SQL query, ship to your door by Friday*).

### Law 2: The Arousal-Valence Dissociation Between Clicking and Sharing (`P14` vs. `P55`)
- **Empirical Mechanism:** Clicking a headline in private and sharing content with peers are governed by opposite affective circuits:
  - **Click-Through Rate (`P14` Robertson et al. 2023, `105,000` Upworthy headlines):** Each negative/threat word increases CTR by **`+2.3%`**, whereas generic positive words reduce CTR.
  - **Viral Sharing (`P55` Berger & Milkman 2012, `6,956` NYT articles):** Positive articles are shared more ($\beta = +0.16, p < .001$), and **physiological arousal** drives transmission: `+1 SD` **Anger (`+34%`, equal to `2.9 hrs` as NYT #1 lead story)**, **Awe (`+30%`)**, **Practical Utility (`+30%`)**, **Interest (`+25%`)**, and **Anxiety (`+21%`)** boost sharing, while low-arousal **Sadness (`-16%`)** kills sharing.
- **Execution Rule (The Threat-to-Awe Pivot):** Open cold headlines/hooks with **1–2 concrete threat/mistake words** to arrest System 1 attention (`+2.3%` CTR/word), then immediately pivot in the subhead/body to **High-Arousal Awe (`+30%`) + Step-by-Step Utility (`+30%`)**. Never close on sad, helpless, or deactivating language (`-16%`).

### Law 3: First-Person Singular `"I"` Agency vs. Corporate `"We"` and Accusatory `"You"` (`P56`)
- **Empirical Mechanism (`Packard, Moore, & McFerran 2018, P56`, `1,276` emails + 5 RCTs):** First-person singular `"I"` (*"I checked your build logs"*, *"I'll ship your replacement today"*) outperforms corporate `"We"` ($M = 5.19$ vs. $4.01, F(1,156) = 24.81, p < .001$) and no-pronoun passives ($M = 4.38, F = 12.16, p < .001$), mediated by **Perceived Empathy** ($b = 0.76, p < .01$) and **Perceived Agency** ($b = 0.68, p = .03$). Customer-referencing `"You"` yields **null lift** in service/persuasion and **backfires** when describing errors or problems.
- **Execution Rule:** Use `"I"` (EN) / `"Tôi / Mình [Tên]"` (VI) whenever an individual specialist, founder, or support agent speaks or acts; reserve `"We / Chúng tôi"` strictly for structural company policies (*"We don't sell user data"*). Never pair `"You / Bạn"` with an error state (*"You entered an invalid card"* $\to$ *"The card number didn't go through—I can retry or send a payment link"*).

### Law 4: Predicate Identity Nouns (`"Be a [Noun]"`) vs. Action Verbs (`"[Verb]"`) (`P58`)
- **Empirical Mechanism (`Bryan, Walton, Rogers, & Dweck 2011, P58`):** Framing a valued behavior as a self-defining noun (*"How important is it to you to **be a voter**?"*) vs. a verb (*"**to vote**"*) increases registration interest from `55.6%` to **`87.5%`** ($p = .041$) and raises official state voter turnout by **`+10.9 to +13.7 percentage points`** (`95.5%` vs. `81.8%` in CA, $p = .018$; `89.9%` vs. `79.0%` in NJ, $p = .027$).
- **Boundary Condition:** Use identity nouns when inviting users to claim an **aspirational, high-status identity** (*"Become an Early Builder"*, *"Trở thành Kiến trúc sư Hệ thống"*) or to inhibit unethical acts (*"Don't be a spammer"*). **Never** use identity nouns when novices face frequent technical failures (*"do an experiment"*, not *"be a scientist"*, so setbacks don't threaten identity).

### Law 5: Empowered Refusal Modals (`"I don't"` vs. `"I can't"`) (`P57`)
- **Empirical Mechanism (`Patrick & Hagtvedt 2012, P57`):** For internal self-regulatory goals, `"I don't"` signals autonomous identity and permanence ($M_{\text{empowerment}} = 6.17$ vs. $5.09, p < .01$), raising healthy choice from `39%` to **`64%`** and increasing 10-day habit persistence **`8×`** (`80%` vs. `10%`, $M = 9.20$ vs. $2.90\text{ days}, F(2,27) = 11.82, p < .001$).
- **Reversal Boundary:** When the constraint is an **external commitment or interpersonal pact** (*"until our Friday release freeze"*), **`"I can't"` outperforms `"I don't"`** ($5.81$ vs. $4.60, F(1,174) = 16.59, p < .001$).

### Law 6: Autonomy-Supportive `"But You Are Free"` (BYAF) Calibration (`P59–P60`)
- **Empirical Mechanism (`Carpenter 2013, P59`; `Fillon et al. 2023, P60`):** Appending an autonomy release (*"But you are free to accept or refuse"*, *"Không ràng buộc—hủy bất kỳ lúc nào chỉ với 1 cú nhấp"*) reduces psychological reactance (`Brehm 1966`), yielding $\text{OR} = 2.20$ ($r = .18$) in immediate face-to-face requests (`P59`), but dropping to $r = .07$ in cold email and shrinking to **$g = 0.11$ (`95% CI [-0.18, 0.40]`)** across Low-RoB experiments (`P60`).
- **Execution Rule:** Deploy autonomy-release microcopy **adjacent to high-reactance commitment gates** (pricing buttons, trial signups, live sales calls) to neutralize pressure—not as a standalone hook.

### Law 7: Surprisal, Uniform Information Density (UID) & Dependency Locality (`P61–P63`)
- **Empirical Mechanism:**
  - **Surprisal (`Levy 2008, P61`):** Word processing time scales with negative log-probability $-\log_2 P(w_i \mid \text{context})$. High-constraint pre-verbal context speeds up final-verb comprehension by **`-51 ms`** (`514 ms` $\to$ `463 ms`).
  - **Uniform Information Density (`Jaeger 2010, P62`):** Comprehension is optimal when information rate is kept uniform ($\chi^2 = 263.0, p < .0001$). Insert explicit syntactic connectors (*that, because, which, rằng, vì*) before high-surprisal clauses; omit them when the next clause is highly predictable.
  - **Dependency Locality (`Gibson 1998, P63`):** Working-memory integration + maintenance cost scales with the number of **new discourse referents** ($n$ nouns + tensed verbs) intervening between a subject and its verb. At $n \ge 3$, comprehension breaks down.
- **Execution Rule:** Keep subject-to-verb distance **$\le 5\text{ words}$ (`0–1` intervening nouns)**; ban center-embedded clauses; right-shift heavy modifiers; place the highest-information payoff word at the **clause-final focus position**.

### Law 8: The Rhetorical Question $\times$ Involvement Crossover (`P64`)
- **Empirical Mechanism (`Petty, Cacioppo, & Heesacker 1981, P64`, $F(1,149) = 14.18, p < .0001$):**
  - **Low Involvement (Cold traffic / passive social scroll) + Strong Proof:** Rhetorical questions **wake up elaboration** and boost persuasion (`+0.61` vs. `+0.04`, $p < .02$).
  - **High Involvement (Pricing page, checkout, technical docs) + Strong Proof:** Readers are already elaborating; rhetorical questions **distract thinking** (`5.87` vs. `4.03`, $p < .004$) and **collapse persuasion by `93%`** (`+0.05` vs. `+0.75`, $p < .01$).
- **Execution Rule:** Use rhetorical questions **only** in top-of-funnel hooks backed by strong proof; use **direct declarative statements** on high-intent product, pricing, and documentation pages.
